Any-Order WoodelfHD

How the v2 symmetry extends from Shapley and Banzhaf values and pairwise interaction values to every cardinality interaction index of any order. It needs \(k+1\) shared diagonals per order, a \(2^k\)-term inclusion-exclusion, and a prefix recurrence that shares those terms.

In short

  • For a CardinalityInteractionIndicesMetric of order \(k\), the M matrices of all \(\binom{D}{k}\) subsets are combinations of \(k+1\) shared matrices. A leaf needs \(k+1\) StrassenLike multiplications per order instead of one per subset.
  • The s vector of a subset is the inclusion-exclusion \(s_S[c]=\sum_{T\subseteq S}s_{|T|}[c^{-T}]\).
  • A prefix recurrence shares those \(2^k\) terms between subsets, so building all the s vectors costs about one vector addition per subset, where v1 needs \(D\).
  • A cost model picks one of two routes: the \(2^k\) terms read at the consumer patterns, or every s vector built over all \(2^D\) patterns and then one lookup per consumer. A route in between, through prefixes of size \(j\), is future work.

1The leaf computation

Take a leaf with weight \(w\) whose root-to-leaf path has \(D\) unique features. The first feature is the most significant bit of a decision pattern. A consumer pattern \(c\) and a background pattern \(b\) define a cube: feature \(i\) is a positive literal when \(c_i=1, b_i=0\) (rule 1), a negated literal when \(c_i=0, b_i=1\) (rule 2), and absent when both are 1 (rule 3).

WoodelfHD keeps only the secondary diagonal. Its row \(q\) is the full cube whose positive literals are the set bits of \(q\) and whose negated literals are the zero bits. Rule 3 follows by linearity: the game of a cube without feature \(i\) is the sum of the games with \(i\) positive and with \(i\) negated. For a subset \(S\), let \(D_S[q]\) be the metric's value of \(S\) on the full cube of \(q\). With \(g\) the prepared, reversed f vector of the leaf, the StrassenLike multiplication is a subset-sum (zeta) transform over the pattern bits:

\[ s_S[c] \;=\; \sum_{q \subseteq c} D_S[q]\, g[q], \qquad\text{and the leaf adds } w\, s_S[c] \text{ to every consumer with pattern } c. \]

Everything below uses one fact: \(s_S\) is linear in \(D_S\). Any way of writing \(D_S\) as a sum of shared diagonals, each restricted to some rows, carries over term by term to \(s_S\).

Notation: \(c^{-T}\) is \(c\) with the bits of the features in \(T\) set to 0, and \(c^{-m}\) is short for \(c^{-\{m\}}\). Over a full \(2^D\) vector \(y\), reading \(y[c^{-m}]\) at every \(c\) copies the half where bit \(m\) is 0 onto both halves, which is a broadcast.

2The v2 symmetry, orders 1 and 2

For Shapley and Banzhaf values, the value of feature \(i\) on a full cube depends only on the cube's number of positive literals \(|q|\), and on whether \(i\) is one of them. So all the features share two diagonals: \(M_+[q] = v_+(|q|)\) and \(M_-[q] = v_-(|q|)\). The diagonal of feature \(i\) is \(M_+\) on the rows where bit \(i\) is set and \(M_-\) on the others, so \(D_i = M_+ + \mathbb 1[i\notin q]\,(M_- - M_+)\). By linearity:

\[ s_i[c] = s_+[c] + s_R\big[c^{-i}\big], \qquad s_R = s_- - s_+ . \]

Pairs need three diagonals, \(M_{++}, M_{+-}, M_{--}\), and the same argument gives the formula of HighDepthWoodelfV2PathToSVectors:

\[ s_{ij}[c] = s_{++}[c] + s_A[c^{-i}] + s_A[c^{-j}] + s_Q[c^{-ij}],\qquad s_A = s_{+-}-s_{++},\quad s_Q = s_{++}-2s_{+-}+s_{--}. \]

3Any order: \(k+1\) shared diagonals

A CardinalityInteractionIndicesMetric gives a subset of size \(k\), on a cube with \(P\) positive and \(Q\) negated literals, a value that depends only on how many of the subset's members are positive. On the full cube of \(q\) (\(P=|q|\), \(Q=D-|q|\)), let \(h_u(q)\) be the value of a \(k\)-subset with \(u\) negated members and \(k-u\) positive ones. In the code this is cardinality_value(D-|q|, |q|, k-u, u), set to 0 where the cube can't hold such a subset. Then

\[ D_S[q] = h_{\,|S\pm q|}(q), \qquad S\pm q = \text{the members of } S \text{ whose bit is 0 in } q. \]

Forward differences

Fix a row \(q\). The value of a \(k\)-subset \(S\) can be decomposed into a sum of terms, one for each set \(T\subseteq S\) of members that are all negated in \(q\): a term for the empty set, one for each negated member, one for each negated pair, and so on. By symmetry, the value of \(S\) depends only on how many of its members are negated, and never on which. Swapping two positive literals of a full cube, or two negated ones, does not change its game, and these indices are symmetric, so they give the same value to every subset with the same number of negated members. Hence the term of \(T\) comes out the same for all the sets of one size, and a single diagonal \(M_t\) holds the term of every set of \(t\) negated members, in every subset.

Pairs, case by case

Take a pair \(S=\{i,j\}\). Its value on row \(q\) is \(h_0\), \(h_1\) or \(h_2\), by how many of \(i, j\) are zero bits of \(q\). Decompose it into \(M_0\) for the empty set, \(M_1\) for each negated member, and \(M_2\) for the two of them when both are negated:

\(q_i\,q_j\)negatedvalue as a sum of terms
11none\(h_0 = M_0\)
01\(i\)\(h_1 = M_0 + M_1\)
10\(j\)\(h_1 = M_0 + M_1\)
00\(i\) and \(j\)\(h_2 = M_0 + M_1 + M_1 + M_2\)

Solving from the top, each case brings in one new unknown:

\[ M_0 = h_0, \qquad M_1 = h_1 - h_0, \qquad M_2 = h_2 - 2h_1 + h_0 . \]

These are the zeroth, first and second forward differences of the sequence \(h_0, h_1, h_2\). \(M_1\) is the term of one negated member, and \(M_2\) the term of a negated pair: the part of \(h_2\) that is not already in the terms of \(\emptyset\), \(\{i\}\) and \(\{j\}\).

By the symmetry, the two middle cases give the same equation, and that is where the sharing comes from. Whichever of \(i, j\) is the negated one, the value is \(h_1\), so one \(M_1\) serves both members. Nothing in \(M_0, M_1, M_2\) names \(i\) or \(j\), so the same three diagonals serve every pair. What sets one pair apart from another is only the rows on which each term is switched on, and those depend only on the bits of its members.

Any order

For a subset of any size \(k\), the terms are found the same way, one size at a time, and \(M_t\) is the \(t\)-th forward difference of \(h\) in \(u\):

\[ M_t[q] \;=\; \sum_{u=0}^{t} (-1)^{t-u}\binom{t}{u}\, h_u(q), \qquad t = 0,\dots,k. \]

Binomial inversion, which here is Newton's forward-difference formula, turns them back into \(h_u = \sum_{t=0}^{u}\binom{u}{t} M_t\).

Derivation of \(h_u = \sum_{t=0}^{u}\binom{u}{t} M_t\)

Write the sum in the definition of \(M_t\) over \(w\), put it in, and swap the two sums, since \(0\le w\le t\le u\):

\[ \sum_{t=0}^{u}\binom{u}{t}M_t \;=\; \sum_{t=0}^{u}\binom{u}{t}\sum_{w=0}^{t}(-1)^{t-w}\binom{t}{w}\,h_w \;=\; \sum_{w=0}^{u}h_w\sum_{t=w}^{u}(-1)^{t-w}\binom{u}{t}\binom{t}{w}. \]

Choosing \(t\) of the \(u\) and then \(w\) of those \(t\) is the same as choosing the \(w\) first and then the other \(t-w\) of the remaining \(u-w\), so \(\binom{u}{t}\binom{t}{w} = \binom{u}{w}\binom{u-w}{t-w}\). With \(s = t-w\) and the binomial theorem, the inner sum is

\[ \sum_{t=w}^{u}(-1)^{t-w}\binom{u}{t}\binom{t}{w} \;=\; \binom{u}{w}\sum_{s=0}^{u-w}(-1)^{s}\binom{u-w}{s} \;=\; \binom{u}{w}\,(1-1)^{u-w}. \]

This is 1 for \(w=u\) and 0 for every \(w<u\), so only \(h_u\) is left. For pairs at \(u=2\): \(M_0+2M_1+M_2 = h_0 + 2(h_1-h_0) + (h_2-2h_1+h_0) = h_2\).

This sum counts the terms that are switched on: a cube in which \(u\) members of \(S\) are negated has exactly \(\binom{u}{t}\) subsets \(T\subseteq S\) of size \(t\) with all their members negated. So

\[ \begin{aligned} D_S[q] &= h_{\,|S\pm q|}(q) = h_u \\ &= \sum_{t}\binom{u}{t}M_t[q] \\ &= \sum_{T \subseteq S} \mathbb 1\big[\text{all of } T \text{ negated in } q\big]\; M_{|T|}[q]. \end{aligned} \]

The inclusion-exclusion

With the shared s vectors \(s_t = \ze(M_t \odot g)\), one StrassenLike multiplication for all \(t\), linearity gives

\[ \begin{aligned} s_S[c] &= \sum_{q\subseteq c} D_S[q]\,g[q] \\ &= \sum_{q\subseteq c}\sum_{T\subseteq S}\mathbb 1[q\cap T=\emptyset]\,M_{|T|}[q]\,g[q] \\ &= \sum_{T\subseteq S}\;\sum_{q\subseteq c^{-T}} M_{|T|}[q]\,g[q] \\ &= \sum_{T \subseteq S} s_{|T|}\big[c^{-T}\big]. \end{aligned} \]

For \(k=1\), \((M_0, M_1) = (M_+,\, M_- - M_+)\), which are the matrices of \(s_+\) and \(s_R\). For \(k=2\), \((M_0,M_1,M_2) = (M_{++},\, M_{+-}-M_{++},\, M_{++}-2M_{+-}+M_{--})\), which are those of \(s_{++}, s_A, s_Q\). A metric of orders \(k_{\min}\dots k_{\max}\) stacks the \(\sum_k (k+1)\) diagonals of its orders and gets every shared s vector from a single StrassenLike multiplication per leaf.

The value 0 used where the cube can't hold a subset is never read. A row \(q\) and a subset \(S\) always pick \(h_u\) with \(u = |S\pm q|\), and that is always feasible. The diagonals are stored as \(M_t\), already differenced at construction, so there is no per-leaf combine step. The covered metrics are GeneralShapleyInteractionValues, GeneralBanzhafInteractionValues, MobiusCoefficients (PDIV of any order), FaithfulShapleyInteractionValues, FaithfulBanzhafInteractionValues and ShapleyTaylorInteractionValues.

4A worked bit example

A path of \(D=6\) features \(f_1\dots f_6\), the subset \(S=\{f_1,f_3,f_5\}\) and the consumer pattern \(c=\)101101. The eight terms of \(s_S[c]\) are:

\(T\)\(c^{-T}\)term
\(\emptyset\)101101\(s_0[101101]\)
\(\{f_1\}\)001101\(s_1[001101]\)
\(\{f_3\}\)100101\(s_1[100101]\)
\(\{f_5\}\)101101\(s_1[101101]\)
\(\{f_1,f_3\}\)000101\(s_2[000101]\)
\(\{f_1,f_5\}\)001101\(s_2[001101]\)
\(\{f_3,f_5\}\)100101\(s_2[100101]\)
\(\{f_1,f_3,f_5\}\)000101\(s_3[000101]\)

Bit \(f_5\) is already 0 in \(c\), so clearing it repeats a pattern. The term still counts, because it reads a different shared vector (\(s_1\) after \(s_0\), and so on).

5Why float64

Each difference \(M_t\) sums \(2^t\) values of \(h\), so \(|M_t| \le 2^t \max|h|\). The terms of the inclusion-exclusion then add up to

\[ \sum_{T\subseteq S}|M_{|T|}| \;\le\; \sum_{t=0}^{k}\binom{k}{t}2^t\,\max|h| \;=\; 3^k \max|h|, \]

while the result is of the order of \(\max|h|\). So float32 loses about \(\log_2 3^k\) bits. The diagonals are float64. Measured on any-order Möbius coefficients at \(D=10\), against a float64 reference:

6The prefix recurrence

Computing every term separately costs \(2^k\) operations per subset and pattern, but subsets that share their first members share most of their terms. Take the members of a subset in path order, and for a prefix \(A\) of a subset define

\[ Z_A^{(r)}[c] \;=\; \sum_{T\subseteq A} s_{|T|+r}\big[c^{-T}\big], \qquad 0 \le r \le k-|A| . \]

Then \(Z_\emptyset^{(r)} = s_r\) and \(s_S = Z_S^{(0)}\). Splitting \(T\) by whether it contains a new member \(m\) gives one broadcast addition per \(r\):

\[ Z_{A\cup\{m\}}^{(r)}[c] = \underbrace{\sum_{T\subseteq A} s_{|T|+r}[c^{-T}]}_{m\notin T} + \underbrace{\sum_{T\subseteq A} s_{|T|+1+r}[c^{-T\cup\{m\}}]}_{m \in T} = Z_A^{(r)}[c] + Z_A^{(r+1)}\big[c^{-m}\big]. \]

As a recursive function, with the members of a prefix in path order:

def Z(A, r, c):                       # Z_A^(r)[c]
    if len(A) == 0:
        return s[r][c]                # the shared s vectors
    m, B = A[-1], A[:-1]              # A = B + [m]
    return Z(B, r, c) + Z(B, r + 1, clear_bit(c, m))

s_S[c] = Z(S, 0, c)

For one subset and one pattern, the recursion reaches its base \(2^k\) times, once per term of the inclusion-exclusion. But every subset that starts with \(A\) calls Z on \(A\), each at its own patterns. So instead, every \(Z_A^{(r)}\) is computed once, bottom-up, as a vector over all \(2^D\) patterns, which turns the recursion into a dynamic program over the prefixes.

Take one subset \(S=(a_1,\dots,a_k)\), its members in path order, and its prefixes \(A_j=(a_1,\dots,a_j)\). Computing \(Z_{A_j}^{(r)}\) needs \(Z_{A_{j-1}}^{(r)}\) and \(Z_{A_{j-1}}^{(r+1)}\), and \(s_S = Z_{A_k}^{(0)}\) needs only \(r=0\) at the last prefix. Going back from \(s_S\), the prefix \(A_j\) is needed for \(r=0,\dots,k-j\). For \(k=3\):

\(r=0\)\(r=1\)\(r=2\)\(r=3\)
\(A_0=\emptyset\)\(s_0\)\(s_1\)\(s_2\)\(s_3\)
\(A_1\)\(Z_{A_1}^{(0)}\)\(Z_{A_1}^{(1)}\)\(Z_{A_1}^{(2)}\)
\(A_2\)\(Z_{A_2}^{(0)}\)\(Z_{A_2}^{(1)}\)
\(A_3=S\)\(s_S\)

Each vector is the one above it plus the one above and to its right, read at \(c^{-a_j}\): \(Z_{A_j}^{(r)} = Z_{A_{j-1}}^{(r)} + Z_{A_{j-1}}^{(r+1)}\big[c^{-a_j}\big]\). So the row of \(A_j\) is computed after the row of \(A_{j-1}\), and one subset alone takes \(3+2+1=6\) additions, \(k(k+1)/2\) in general, where the inclusion-exclusion takes \(2^k-1\).

A row is shared by every subset that starts with its prefix, so the dynamic program computes each row once, level by level, a level being the rows of all the prefixes of one size. Level \(i\) has \(N_i=\binom{D-k+i}{i}\) rows, one per prefix of size \(i\) that leaves room for the other \(k-i\) members, of \(k-i+1\) vectors each. Building every s vector over all \(2^D\) patterns takes

\[ \Big(\sum_{i=1}^{k}(k-i+1)\,N_i\Big)\cdot 2^D \;\text{additions, against}\quad \binom{D}{k}2^k\cdot 2^D \text{ term by term, and } \binom{D}{k}\,D\cdot 2^D \text{ in v1.} \]

At \(D=14, k=3\) these are 556, 2,912 and 5,096 vectors of \(2^{14}\) values. While \(k\) is small compared with \(D\), most of the work is the last level, one vector per subset, so the dynamic program costs about one addition per subset.

Each level is computed in batches. The prefixes of a level are ordered by their last member, so the prefixes that a member \(m\) extends are the leading ones. One NumPy broadcast per (level, member) computes all their extensions by \(m\), for all \(r\): \(O(D\cdot k)\) calls per leaf instead of one per subset. When a level would exceed SUBSETS_BATCH_ELEMENTS values, the prefixes are first split by their next member.

7Future work: the prefix size \(j\)

Not in the code

The code has only the two ends of this route, \(j=0\) and \(j=k\), and picks the cheaper by \(\mathrm{cost}(0)\) against \(\mathrm{cost}(k)\) below (should_use_inclusion_exclusion). A prototype of the whole route was up to 3 times faster with fewer consumers than about \(2^D\), about 2 times at \(n=10^4\) on paths of 14 to 16 features, and no faster with \(10^5\) consumers or more, where the code always builds the full s vectors. We left it out for now, for simplicity: it is a third route with its own bookkeeping, for a speedup at small \(n\) only. The measurements are at the end of this section.

The dynamic program doesn't have to run to the last level. Tabulate only the levels \(1..j\) over all \(2^D\) patterns, and get every \(s_S\) at the consumer patterns by running the recursion of section 6 with its base at level \(j\) instead of the empty prefix:

def Z(A, r, c):                       # route j
    if len(A) == j:
        return table[A][r][c]         # level j, tabulated over all 2^D patterns
    m, B = A[-1], A[:-1]
    return Z(B, r, c) + Z(B, r + 1, clear_bit(c, m))

Write \(S\) as its first \(j\) members \(A\) and the other \(k-j\) members \(R\). The recursion unrolls the members of \(R\), and each of them either keeps \(c\) and \(r\), or clears its bit and raises \(r\) by one. So the calls that reach level \(j\) are one per set \(U\subseteq R\) of cleared members:

\[ s_S[c] = Z_S^{(0)}[c] = \sum_{U \subseteq R} Z_A^{(|U|)}\big[c^{-U}\big] \qquad (2^{k-j}\text{ lookups into level } j). \]

Both ends are special cases. \(j=0\) tabulates nothing and unrolls everything down to the base \(Z_\emptyset^{(r)} = s_r\), which is the inclusion-exclusion of section 3. \(j=k\) tabulates every level, which is the full dynamic program of section 6, and reads every \(s_S\) once. In the prototype, the unrolled part was the inclusion-exclusion, _inclusion_exclusion_s_vectors_at, run on one prefix's rows, over the completions \(R\) of \(A\). Those completions are a contiguous suffix of the lexicographic subset masks, so it needed no new mask code.

Choosing \(j\)

With \(n\) consumers, the prototype took the \(j\) of the lowest

\[ \mathrm{cost}(j) = 2^D\sum_{i=1}^{j}(k-i+1)N_i \;+\; \frac{\binom{D}{k}\,2^{k-j}\,n}{\rho(D)} , \]

where \(\rho(D)\) is how many lookups cost about as much as one addition over the patterns. It was benchmarked as the crossover between \(j=0\) and \(j=k\): about 3 for \(D\le 10\), and about 1.5 for deeper paths, where the lookups into longer vectors miss the cache more often. Each cost is a line in \(n\) whose slope falls as \(j\) grows, so the chosen \(j\) only grows with \(n\), from 0 to \(k\).

8The neighbor leaf

With the neighbor leaf trick, the right sibling's f is the neighbor vector of the left leaf's f, and its consumer patterns are \(c \oplus 1\), the last bit flipped. Both leaves go through the recurrence together, stacked on a leading axis of the same arrays. At \(j=k\) the sibling's s vectors are folded in before the lookup: \(s_S[c] + s'_S[c\oplus 1]\) is the element-pair swap of \(s'_S\) added to \(s_S\), so every consumer is looked up once. At \(j=0\) the sibling's terms are read at \(c\oplus 1\).

For one order \(k\), a path with \(D\) unique features and \(n\) consumers:

StrassenLike multiplication\(\binom{D}{k}\cdot D\, 2^D\)\((k+1)\cdot D\, 2^D\)
s vectors of the subsets(in the multiplication)\(\min\big(\mathrm{cost}(0), \mathrm{cost}(k)\big)\), at most about \(\binom{D}{k}(2^D + n)\)
consumer values\(\binom{D}{k}\cdot n\)\(\binom{D}{k}\cdot n\) at \(j=k\), \(\binom{D}{k}\cdot 2^k n\) at \(j=0\)

v2 saves the factor \(\binom{D}{k}/(k+1)\) in the multiplication and in memory. Building the s vectors costs about one addition per subset and pattern where v1 spends \(D\), and with few consumers v2 doesn't build them at all. It cannot beat the output itself: when \(n\gg 2^D\), both are bound by the \(\binom{D}{k}\,n\) values they must write.

11A remark for the paper

Remark

Let \(\phi\) be an interaction index of order \(k\) whose value on a cube depends only on how many members of the subset are positive literals. This holds for the Shapley, Banzhaf, Möbius, faithful Shapley and Banzhaf, and Shapley-Taylor indices. Then each of the \(\binom{D}{k}\) M matrices of a path is a sum of row restrictions of \(k+1\) shared matrices, the forward differences of \(\phi\) in the number of negated members. WoodelfHD needs \(k+1\) StrassenLike multiplications per order instead of \(\binom{D}{k}\), and \(O(k\,2^D)\) memory instead of \(O(\binom{D}{k}2^D)\).

The s vector of every subset follows by the inclusion-exclusion \(s_S[c]=\sum_{T\subseteq S}s_{|T|}[c^{-T}]\). Evaluated along subset prefixes, this costs \(\sum_{i}(k-i+1)\binom{D-k+i}{i}\) vector additions, about one per subset while \(k\ll D\), against \(D\) per subset in v1.
